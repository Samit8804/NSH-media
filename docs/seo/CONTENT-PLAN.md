## 3. Content principles

### Accuracy

- Use only verified services, prices, technologies, and contact details.
- Do not state a founding year, team size, location, client count, award, rating, or result unless the business supplies evidence.
- Do not describe a capability as a standalone service if it is not in the service catalogue.
- Do not promise rankings, traffic, revenue, timelines, or support terms that are not agreed in writing.

### Usefulness

- Every page should answer a real buyer question, not repeat a keyword.
- Prefer concrete checklists, decision criteria, and examples over generic marketing language.
- Explain limitations as clearly as capabilities.

### Structure

- One H1 per page.
- Question-style H2s where they match real queries.
- A direct answer in the first 40–60 words under each question heading.
- Short paragraphs, lists, and tables where they improve scanning.
- A visible “last updated” date on articles.

### Proof

- Case studies require a real client, a real brief, real technologies, and outcomes the client permits the business to publish.
- Testimonials require a real quote and permission to publish the person's name and company.
- Team bios require real names, roles, and photos.
- Until then, use process and service explanations rather than implied proof.

## 5. Article briefs — Priority 1

### Brief 1: What a Website Design Project Includes

- **Search intent:** A business owner wants to know what they receive and what is expected of them.
- **Direct answer:** A website design project typically covers discovery, site structure, wireframes, responsive page designs, a reusable design system, and developer handoff. Explain that content, photography, and third-party integrations may be separate inputs.
- **Sections:** Discovery; information architecture; wireframes; visual design; design system; review rounds; handoff; what is not included.
- **Internal links:** `/services/web-design`, `/pricing`, `/portfolio`, `/contact`.
- **Do not claim:** a fixed number of revisions, a guaranteed timeline, or free extras unless confirmed.

### Brief 2: How Long Does It Take to Build a Website?

- **Search intent:** Timeline planning before contacting a studio.
- **Direct answer:** The published FAQ says most projects take 4–8 weeks from kickoff to launch, depending on complexity and scope. Attribute this to the FAQ and explain what affects the timeline.
- **Sections:** Typical phases; what speeds a project up; what causes delays; how content and feedback affect timing; how timelines are agreed.
- **Internal links:** `/faq`, `/services/web-development`, `/contact`.
- **Do not claim:** a guaranteed launch date or a fixed timeline for every project.

### Brief 3: How Much Does a Website Cost?

- **Search intent:** Budget qualification.
- **Direct answer:** Published packages start at $499. Final cost depends on scope, features, and content. Link to `/pricing` for current starting prices.
- **Sections:** What changes the price; package comparison; custom and e-commerce scope; ongoing support; how to request a quote.
- **Internal links:** `/pricing`, `/services/web-design`, `/services/web-development`, `/services/ecommerce`, `/contact`.
- **Do not claim:** hidden fees, discounts, or a guaranteed price range beyond the published packages.

### Brief 4: Web Design vs. Web Development

- **Search intent:** Understanding roles before hiring.
- **Direct answer:** Web design defines structure, interface, and visual system. Web development turns that design into a working, responsive, maintainable website or application.
- **Sections:** Definitions; overlap; deliverables; when a project needs both; how handoff works; questions to ask a studio.
- **Internal links:** `/services/web-design`, `/services/web-development`, `/portfolio`.
- **Do not claim:** that the business offers separate branding, SEO, or maintenance services.

### Brief 5: Custom Store vs. Shopify vs. WooCommerce

- **Search intent:** Platform selection for an online store.
- **Direct answer:** The choice depends on catalogue size, customization needs, payment and inventory requirements, internal capacity, and long-term maintenance. The service FAQ confirms custom builds plus Shopify and WooCommerce experience.
- **Sections:** Decision criteria; custom store strengths and trade-offs; Shopify strengths and trade-offs; WooCommerce strengths and trade-offs; migration considerations; how to scope the project.
- **Internal links:** `/services/ecommerce`, `/pricing`, `/contact`, `/industries/e-commerce`.
- **Do not claim:** platform pricing, transaction fees, or performance guarantees without current verified data.

### Brief 6: What a Business Website Should Include Before Launch

- **Search intent:** Launch readiness.
- **Direct answer:** A business website needs clear positioning, essential pages, working contact paths, responsive layouts, accessible navigation, metadata, analytics, and a tested deployment.
- **Sections:** Content; navigation; conversion paths; mobile behavior; accessibility; technical SEO; analytics; legal pages; launch checklist.
- **Internal links:** `/services/web-development`, `/faq`, `/contact`.
- **Do not claim:** that every project includes every item; distinguish standard scope from optional work.

## 6. AEO template for articles

Use this structure for question-led content:

1. **H1:** Match the article's promise, not a keyword string.
2. **Opening answer:** 40–60 words that answer the title question directly.
3. **Context:** Who this applies to and what can change the answer.
4. **H2 question sections:** Each starts with the question and a direct answer.
5. **Checklist or comparison table:** Make the content easy to extract.
6. **NSH Media Services context:** One short section describing the relevant verified service and linking to it.
7. **FAQ:** Two to four questions, visible on the page, with `FAQPage` schema only if the answers are visible.
8. **CTA:** One primary action (start a project or view pricing) and one secondary action (read a related service page).
9. **Date:** Visible published and updated dates.

Avoid opening with “In today's fast-paced digital world.” Avoid unsupported superlatives.

## 7. GEO template

Generative engines need unambiguous, attributable statements. Every article should include:

- **Entity sentence:** “NSH Media Services is a digital design and development studio offering web design, web development, and e-commerce services.”
- **Scope statement:** What the business does and does not offer as a standalone service.
- **First-party facts:** Canonical URL, contact email, published services, and verified technologies.
- **Definitions:** Define terms such as “responsive design,” “technical SEO foundations,” and “custom e-commerce” in plain language.
- **Source boundaries:** Attribute timelines and prices to the FAQ or pricing page; do not present estimates as universal facts.
- **Update discipline:** Refresh when services, prices, or processes change.
- **No invented proof:** Do not imply clients, results, or awards that are not published.

The public `llms.txt` repeats the same entity facts and explicitly tells AI systems not to infer an address, phone number, founding date, team size, awards, ratings, or results.

## 9. Publishing workflow

1. **Brief:** Confirm the query, audience, primary service link, and required inputs.
2. **Draft:** Answer the question first; add context and examples.
3. **Fact-check:** Verify every company claim, price, timeline, platform, and technology.
4. **Edit:** Remove superlatives, filler, and implied proof.
5. **Build:** Add the post to the CMS with title, excerpt, category, tags, image alt text, and author.
6. **Validate:** Check one H1, unique title/description, canonical, internal links, and JSON-LD.
7. **Publish:** Set `published: true`; the sitemap reads published posts from the database.
8. **Index:** Submit the URL in Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools after deployment.
9. **Review:** Update after 30 days and every six months, or when services or prices change.

Recommended cadence: two articles per month for the first three months, then reassess based on capacity and Search Console data. Do not publish on a schedule the business cannot sustain.

Track after deployment:

- Organic sessions to service, pricing, portfolio, and contact pages.

- Referrals from AI assistants where the referrer is available.

Do not evaluate success on word count or publishing volume. Evaluate whether pages answer questions and create qualified enquiries.
